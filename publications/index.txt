With the rapid accumulation of human sensing data (HSD), empirical research in this domain has experienced explosive growth in recent years. However, the existing literature lacks a comprehensive and systematic review that synthesises the research trends, core patterns, limitations, and future directions within this interdisciplinary field. Following the PRISMA guidelines, this review systematically searched Scopus, Web of Science, and PubMed, ultimately including 472 eligible empirical studies. Leveraging large language model (LLM)-assisted analysis, a situational context framework and thematic clustering were applied. This review is positioned as a structured thematic synthesis and research-landscape mapping. The findings reveal interdisciplinary characteristics and a spatiotemporal agglomeration in East Asia. The final synthesis is organised into four core clusters: (A) sensory neurophysiological mechanisms, (B) data-driven perceptual quantification, (C) spatiotemporal perception equity, and (D) perception-based planning discussion. Specifically, Cluster A reports cross-modal synergy, compensation or interference in a subset of interaction studies, mainly in micro-scale or controlled settings; these are patterns in that subset, not field-wide mechanisms. Cluster B has facilitated a shift from traditional subjective scales toward the integration of user-generated content (UGC) and physiological signals for quantification. Cluster C highlights heterogeneity in the distribution of five-sense perception across space, time and social groups. Cluster D underscores planning-oriented studies that draw on five-sense evidence to inform design guidance rather than documented planning outcomes.
